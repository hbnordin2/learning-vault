# Knowlton Mangels & Squire 1996

Knowlton, B. J., Mangels, J. A., & Squire, L. R. (1996). A neostriatal habit learning system in humans. *Science, 273*(5280), 1399–1402. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.273.5280.1399

The classic *double* dissociation between procedural and declarative memory, using the probabilistic-classification ("weather prediction") task. Amnesic patients (medial-temporal/hippocampal damage) learned the probabilistic classification skill at a normal rate but could not remember the training episodes; Parkinson's patients (basal-ganglia/neostriatal dysfunction) showed the *reverse* — impaired skill (habit) learning with intact declarative memory of the task. Because each capacity can be knocked out while the other is spared, in both directions, this is strong evidence that procedural/habit learning and declarative memory depend on different brain systems — the gold-standard form of evidence for "knowing how and knowing that are different things."
