# Brunmair & Richter 2019

Brunmair, M., & Richter, T. (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. *Psychological Bulletin, 145*(11), 1029–1052. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209

The definitive meta-analysis of [[Interleaving|interleaving]] (59 studies, 238 effect sizes). Overall **g ≈ 0.42**, but the average hides enormous heterogeneity that the title flags: the effect is strong for **inductive category learning** (g ≈ 0.67), modest for **mathematics** (g ≈ 0.34), and **non-significant for expository texts and tastes/flavours.** Material similarity is the key moderator — interleaving helps when the task is to discriminate between confusable categories (forcing attention onto between-category differences and which rule applies) and not when categories are already distinct or the goal is within-category commonality. The corrective to the popular "always interleave" rule.
