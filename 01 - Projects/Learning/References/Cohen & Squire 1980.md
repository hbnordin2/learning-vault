# Cohen & Squire 1980

Cohen, N. J., & Squire, L. R. (1980). Preserved learning and retention of pattern-analyzing skill in amnesia: Dissociation of knowing how and knowing that. *Science, 210*(4466), 207–210. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7414331

The empirical naming of the knowing-how / knowing-that distinction. Amnesic patients learned a mirror-reading skill — reading mirror-reversed text, which gets faster with practice — at a *normal* rate and retained the improvement over time, while showing severely impaired memory for the specific words they had read and for the training sessions themselves. The procedural skill ("knowing how") was acquired and kept; the declarative memory of acquiring it ("knowing that") was absent. Foundational evidence that skill learning and fact memory are separable systems, not two ends of one process.
