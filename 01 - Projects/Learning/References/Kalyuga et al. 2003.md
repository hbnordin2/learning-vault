# Kalyuga et al. 2003

Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The expertise reversal effect. *Educational Psychologist, 38*(1), 23–31. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4

Instructional support that helps novices — worked examples, integrated diagrams, extra explanation — becomes useless or *harmful* as expertise grows, because the provided guidance is redundant information the expert must reconcile against internal knowledge, adding extraneous cognitive load. A textbook aptitude–treatment (crossover) interaction, replicated across split-attention, redundancy, modality, and worked-example paradigms, and into classrooms (e.g., Salden et al., 2010). The basis of the guidance-fading prescription: full worked example → completion problems → independent problem solving, as competence increases.
