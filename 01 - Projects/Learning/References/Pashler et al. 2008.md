# Pashler et al. 2008

Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008). Learning styles: Concepts and evidence. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9*(3), 105–119. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x

The definitive refutation of learning styles. Isolates the only instructionally meaningful version — the **meshing hypothesis** (the *best* method depends on the learner's style), which can only be confirmed by a specific crossover interaction in a randomised design — and shows the required studies are almost never run, and the few adequate ones contradict it. There is no credible evidence that matching teaching to a learner's "style" improves learning. The flagship neuromyth, still endorsed by the large majority of educators worldwide (Dekker et al., 2012; Macdonald et al., 2017).
