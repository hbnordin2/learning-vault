# Gick & Holyoak 1983

Gick, M. L., & Holyoak, K. J. (1983). Schema induction and analogical transfer. *Cognitive Psychology, 15*(1), 1–38. (Companion: Gick & Holyoak, 1980, *Cognitive Psychology, 12*, 306–355.)

The radiation/fortress analogy studies — the canonical demonstration that spontaneous far transfer is poor and comparison is the lever. Given an analogous story then a structurally identical problem, only ~30% of people spontaneously applied it (≈75% once explicitly hinted). Crucially, trying to induce the abstract schema from a *single* example mostly failed, but giving *two* analogues and asking learners to describe their similarity reliably induced a "convergence schema" as a byproduct — and the closer their similarity description came to the abstract schema, the more they solved the target. **Abstraction via comparison is the active ingredient, not mere exposure.**
