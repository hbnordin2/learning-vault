# Kirschner Sweller & Clark 2006

Kirschner, P. A., Sweller, J., & Clark, R. E. (2006). Why minimal guidance during instruction does not work: An analysis of the failure of constructivist, discovery, problem-based, experiential, and inquiry-based teaching. *Educational Psychologist, 41*(2), 75–86. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1

Argues from human cognitive architecture (limited working memory) that minimal-guidance instruction fails: a novice with no schema burns working memory searching the problem space instead of building schemas, so little is encoded. For novices, worked examples and explicit guidance beat unguided discovery. The key boundary (its own mechanism, the expertise-reversal effect) is that the advantage *recedes as prior knowledge rises*. The substantive rebuttal (Hmelo-Silver, Duncan & Chinn, 2007) is that well-designed problem-based learning is heavily scaffolded, so the critique lands on *unguided* discovery, not scaffolded inquiry; both sides agree prior knowledge is the moderator.
