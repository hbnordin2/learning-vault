# Bisra et al. 2018

Bisra, K., Liu, Q., Nesbit, J. C., Salimi, F., & Winne, P. H. (2018). Inducing self-explanation: A meta-analysis. *Educational Psychology Review, 30*, 703–725. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x

Meta-analysis of prompting learners to explain material to themselves: 69 effect sizes from 64 reports (~5,917 participants), overall random-effects **g = 0.55** — "potentially powerful," and one of the better-supported comprehension techniques. The key moderator is generative: the benefit is larger when learners *generate* the explanation themselves and is *reduced when the correct explanation is also provided.* This converges with the worked-example literature (adding self-explanation prompts can cancel the worked-example benefit): self-explanation competes with provision rather than stacking on top of it.
