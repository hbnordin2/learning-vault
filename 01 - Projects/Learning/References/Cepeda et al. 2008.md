# Cepeda et al. 2008

Cepeda, N. J., Vul, E., Rohrer, D., Wixted, J. T., & Pashler, H. (2008). Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention. *Psychological Science, 19*(11), 1095–1102. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x

The study behind the actionable form of the [[The spacing effect|spacing effect]] (N > 1,350, gaps up to 3.5 months, tests up to a year out). The optimal gap between study sessions *grows* as the target retention interval grows, but as a *proportion* of the retention interval it *shrinks* — roughly 10–20% of the delay for short horizons, falling toward ~5–10% for a one-year horizon. Practically: review across days to remember for a month, at ~3–4-week intervals to remember for a year. The function is broad and forgiving — there is no single magic interval, and any substantial spacing beats massing.
