# Bjork Dunlosky & Kornell 2013

Bjork, R. A., Dunlosky, J., & Kornell, N. (2013). Self-regulated learning: Beliefs, techniques, and illusions. *Annual Review of Psychology, 64*, 417–444. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143823

The authoritative review of how learners regulate (and mis-regulate) their own learning. Central claims: **learning ≠ performance** — conditions that improve long-term retention often depress performance during study; **desirable difficulties** (spacing, interleaving, generation, testing) impair acquisition but enhance durable learning; and learners suffer **illusions of competence**, misreading the fluency of rereading and massed study as mastery. The honest caveat the review itself states: difficulties are desirable *only if surmountable* — for low-knowledge learners or high-load material they become undesirable.
