# Interleaving

Interleaving is practising different topics or problem types mixed together, rather than in separate blocks (ABCABC instead of AAABBBCCC). It improves long-term retention and, most distinctively, the ability to *choose the right approach* when the problem does not indicate which one applies. It is rated *moderate-utility* by Dunlosky et al. (2013). It is the most over-generalised of the core mechanisms: effective in a narrow band of conditions, roughly neutral elsewhere.

## Effect size, and why the average misleads

The meta-analysis is **Brunmair & Richter (2019)** — 59 studies, *Similarity Matters* — with an overall **g ≈ 0.42**. That average hides large heterogeneity; the moderators matter far more than the headline figure:

- **Inductive category learning** (e.g., learning to classify painters by style, or bird species): **g ≈ 0.67** — interleaving's strongest and cleanest domain.
- **Mathematics problem-solving:** **g ≈ 0.34** (Rohrer & Taylor, 2007); Rohrer's randomised *classroom* trials show larger gains on delayed tests.
- **Expository texts and tastes/flavours:** non-significant — interleaving does not reliably help, and these null results are routinely dropped from popular accounts.

## The category-discrimination boundary

The mechanism explains the pattern. Interleaving helps when the task is to **discriminate between confusable categories** — mixing examples directs attention onto the *between-category differences* and onto selecting which rule or schema applies. Blocking is better when the categories are already distinct, or when the goal is to absorb what the members of one category have *in common* (blocked study highlights shared features). Interleaving is therefore best understood as a **discrimination-training tool**, not a universal instruction to mix everything. Material similarity is the key moderator, which is the point of the meta-analysis's title.

One confound to note: interleaving inherently *spaces* practice on each topic, so part of its benefit overlaps with [[The spacing effect|spacing]]. Well-controlled studies attempt to separate the two, but their contributions are not fully disentangled.

## Performance during practice vs. at test

Interleaving reliably *lowers* performance and *lowers* confidence during practice while *raising* performance at test — a prototypical [[Desirable difficulties|desirable difficulty]]. Learners mis-predict accordingly: in Kornell & Bjork (2008), people learned painters' styles better under interleaving yet judged the blocked schedule to have helped more. A study schedule that feels disorganised and harder is weak evidence that it is working — see [[The metacognitive trap]].

## How to use it

- Interleave **related-but-confusable** categories and problem types, where the relevant skill is picking the right one: different objection types and the response each calls for; different valuation or pricing methods and when each applies; different ad formats and which fits a brief. The value is in training the *selection*, which blocked practice never exercises.
- **Block first, then interleave.** When a skill is genuinely new and no version of it can yet be executed, block it until it can be done once; interleaving confusable variants before they can be told apart produces confusion (the surmountability caveat in [[Desirable difficulties]]).
- Interleaving unrelated material yields little; for distinct categories or single-topic facts the effect is small or absent, and [[The spacing effect|spacing]] and [[The testing effect|retrieval]] do the work there.
