# Signaling and advance organizers

Signaling refers to any text feature that highlights the structure or importance of content without adding new propositional content: headers, topic sentences, bold text, numbering, preview sentences, and typographic emphasis. The function is to help readers build a **macrostructure** — a mental representation of how the text is organized — which supports both recall and comprehension.

## Headings

Lorch, Lemarie, and colleagues developed the SARA framework (Signal Available Relevant Accessible information) for analyzing how text signals work. Research shows headings improve topic recall and help low-comprehension readers more than high-comprehension readers. Lemarie et al. (2008) found that **visually distinctive signals** (bold typeface, spatial separation) are more effective than discursive signals (preview sentences) because they are more easily accessible during scanning. The mechanism: headings serve as retrieval cues for the content beneath them, similar to the cue-target structure of a flashcard — see [[Cue-dependent recall]].

## Advance organizers

Ausubel (1960) defined advance organizers as "appropriately relevant and inclusive introductory materials at a higher level of abstraction, generality, and inclusiveness." The idea is that providing a conceptual scaffold before the details gives the reader schema-like structure to anchor incoming information — see [[Prior knowledge and comprehension]].

The evidence is genuinely mixed. Luiten et al. (1980) found effects that were "small but facilitate learning in all content areas and with individuals of all grade and ability levels." But Clark and Bean (1980) found "little support for the efficacy of advance organizers," Gillies (1984) found increased initial learning but no long-term retention benefit, and Kumagai (2013) characterized three decades of literature as generating "a mixed review." Concrete organizers (including pictorial components) outperform abstract ones, particularly for younger learners (Townsend & Clarihew, 1989).

The confident practitioner advice ("always use advance organizers") overstates what the evidence supports. What is better supported: structural signals help readers who would not spontaneously construct a macrostructure, and the benefit is larger for these readers than for skilled readers who construct macrostructure on their own.

## Evidence tier

- **Headings improve topic recall**: experimental, but effect sizes are modest and sample-dependent. Moderate.
- **SARA framework**: theoretical model with supporting experimental evidence. Moderate.
- **Advance organizers facilitate learning**: meta-analytic results are mixed. The Luiten et al. (1980) meta-analysis found small effects; subsequent decades have not resolved the inconsistency. Low-moderate confidence for the general claim; moderate confidence for the narrower claim that concrete organizers help low-knowledge readers.
- **"Always use advance organizers"**: practitioner consensus that outpaces the empirical base.
