# Deliberate practice in fuzzy domains

How to build skill in sales, go-to-market, and market selection — domains where the conditions that make [[Deliberate practice|deliberate practice]] work (clear goals, immediate unambiguous feedback, repeatable tasks) are partly or wholly absent. In these domains the science functions less as a method than as a warning, and the task is to turn that warning into a discipline: manufacture the feedback the domain does not supply.

## Why more repetition fails here

Deliberate practice explains under 1% of performance variance in the professions ([[Deliberate practice|Macnamara et al., 2014]]) — not because practice is useless, but because professional environments lack the conditions it requires. The claim that 10,000 sales calls produces an elite performer is contradicted by the best evidence.

The reason is Kahneman & Klein's (2009) account of when experience builds genuine skill. Skilled intuition requires both a high-validity environment (stable, regular relationships between cues and outcomes) and prolonged practice with rapid, unambiguous feedback. In low-validity, noisy, delayed-feedback environments — market prediction, long-range strategy, fundraising timing, long-cycle enterprise deals — experience does not build expertise. People gain confidence without accuracy. This is [[The metacognitive trap]] at the skill level: subjective confidence and felt experience are not diagnostic of actual ability.

## Sorting work: which pockets reward repetition, which mislead

Sort work into two kinds and use a different tool for each.

**Higher-validity, fast-feedback pockets — real skill can form. Use deliberate practice.**
Running the same SDR script and watching reply and booking rates; iterating ad creative against same-week CTR and cost-per-lead; demo delivery; the repeatable middle of a defined sales motion. These have stable-enough cue-to-outcome links and quick feedback, so they fit the textbook recipe: stretch goals, a rubric, recorded calls reviewed against it, a coach, and repetition with refinement. Invest repetitions here, where they compound.

**Low-validity, slow/noisy pockets — experience will mislead. Switch tools.**
Which market to enter, large positioning bets, fundraising timing. Repetitions here buy confidence, not calibration. Engineer the feedback the environment denies:

- **Pre-register every consequential bet as a dated probabilistic prediction** — "60% chance this channel hits CAC < $X by Q3" — then score it later. This is the reliable way to manufacture calibration feedback and to remove hindsight bias, and it is what improved forecasters in Tetlock's Good Judgment work.
- **Run process-focused post-mortems and pre-mortems.** Judge the reasoning given what was known at the time, not the noisy outcome — a good decision can have a bad result in a low-validity domain, and rewarding outcomes teaches the wrong lesson.
- **Recruit red-teamers who attack the reasoning** rather than a "coach," since no one can have valid intuitions in a low-validity domain either.

## Summary

In fuzzy domains, raw experience and confidence are decoupled from accuracy and should not be treated as evidence of skill. Rely on the feedback loop that is engineered rather than the subjective sense of competence. Put repetitions where feedback is fast and the environment is stable; apply the forecasting-and-scoring discipline where it is not.
