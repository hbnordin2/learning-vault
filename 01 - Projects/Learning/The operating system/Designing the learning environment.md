# Designing the learning environment

Changing the situation is one researched form of self-control. The useful question is what the setting makes easy to do: study, check messages, start chores, or switch to something else. This supplies a missing step before [[01 - Projects/Learning/The operating system/The operating system|The operating system]]'s study techniques.

## The finding: intervene before the temptation

Duckworth, Gendler & Gross (2016) distinguish **situation selection** (choosing where to work) from **situation modification** (changing the place already occupied). Their review proposes that acting before a tempting impulse develops can reduce the need to resist it later. It is a theoretical synthesis, not proof that environmental strategies always outperform every other strategy. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615623247).

Duckworth et al. (2016) also ran field experiments with high-school and college students. Instructions to modify the situation improved self-reported academic goal attainment over the following week relative to instructions to resist impulses or a control condition. The college study also found reduced temptation. These were short interventions with self-report outcomes; they do not establish long-term learning gains or effects in every adult workplace. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000062).

## What this supports about leaving the house

**Application of those findings:** if working at home repeatedly means switching between study, chores, messages, and entertainment, choosing another setting is a reasonable way to change the competing opportunities. A library, office, or other suitable workspace can be an implementation of situation selection. Removing a game controller, closing messaging software, or arranging an interruption-free period at home implements situation modification.

The cited experiments did **not** randomly compare home, café, office, and library. They do not support a universal claim that working outside the house improves focus, that novelty itself causes the benefit, or that a particular venue is necessary. The proposed move is grounded in changing the identified distraction; the venue recommendation is an application, not a separately established effect.

Example: a learner repeatedly abandons a technical chapter to open a game. One environmental change is to read on a device without that game installed. Moving the same gaming laptop to a café while keeping everything open does not implement that particular change. This is an illustration of the distinction, not an experimental result.

## Notifications and phone presence are different claims

Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert (2015) experimentally found that receiving phone notifications disrupted an attention task even without interacting with the phone. Silencing nonessential alerts during study is a direct practical application. The experiment measured attention, not long-term course attainment. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000100).

The stronger claim that a silent phone's **mere presence** reliably drains cognitive capacity is unsettled. Hartanto et al. (2024) pooled 33 studies and found no significant overall effect, *d* = −0.02. Putting a phone elsewhere can restrict checking; it should not be justified here as a proven universal brain-capacity boost. [Meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1037/tmb0000123).

## A stable place is a starting cue, not a mastery test

Environmental context can affect recall, with important boundary conditions (Smith & Vela, 2001). Successfully remembering something in its original setting does not establish that it can be recalled elsewhere. [Meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196157).

**Application:** a repeatable setup can support starting, while occasional unaided practice in the intended use context checks whether the knowledge is usable there. Habit initiation and [[Transfer of learning|transfer]] answer different questions. No requirement to rotate study locations follows from this evidence.
