# The operating system

The applied layer: how to take a topic — go-to-market, sales engineering, market selection, a new technical stack — and learn it so it sticks and can be used on a live problem. This is the part that turns the mechanisms into a workflow for a self-directed adult without a structured course.

Start with [[A learning session from start to finish]] for the concrete sequence. This map combines research on initiating work, instructional guidance, practice, retention, and feedback. Those components have different evidence bases; the combined workflow is a synthesis, not a validated package. [[Learning evidence and open questions]] records its boundaries.

## Before the study techniques

- **Arrange the situation:** [[Designing the learning environment]]. Address the competing activity or interruption. Leaving home is one possible application, not a universal prescription.
- **Specify the start:** [[Starting and sustaining a learning habit]]. State the cue and intended action.
- **Choose the capability and check the current level:** [[Planning and monitoring learning]]. Determine whether instruction, recall, execution, or transfer is the immediate problem.
- **Handle what interrupts continuation:** [[Attention, interruptions, and breaks]] and [[Motivation, avoidance, and difficulty]]. These notes separate experimentally supported effects from practical extrapolations.

## Match the tool to the layer

Knowledge comes in layers, and a different tool governs each. A common mistake is using a fact tool on a skill problem (carding toward competence) or a skill approach on a fact problem (relying on repetition alone to fix recall):

- **Facts / declarative knowledge** (formulas, definitions, named frameworks, benchmark numbers, pricing) → [[Spaced repetition systems]]. Distinguish the evidence for spacing and retrieval from evidence for a particular app or algorithm.
- **Understanding / how it connects** (why a GTM motion works, how a market's incentives fit together) → [[Learning from a book or course]], [[Elaboration and self-explanation]], and suitable examples. The relevant test is an explanation or application, not only recognition.
- **Skill / judgment** (running the campaign, the sales call, the actual design or strategy) → [[Learning to do, not just know]], [[Project-based and just-in-time learning]], and [[Feedback, help, and learning with others]]. [[Deliberate practice in fuzzy domains]] covers the additional difficulty of unreliable feedback.

## The loop

1. **Learn with appropriate guidance**, then retrieve and self-explain — use instruction or worked examples when the method is unfamiliar; close the source and attempt an explanation or answer when there is something to retrieve. [[Learning from a book or course]].
2. **Synthesise** connection-heavy material by building a concept map from memory, then checking it. Use a Zettelkasten or notes system for idea output, not for memory; it is not a validated retention tool.
3. **Memorise** only the genuinely atomic facts, in [[Spaced repetition systems|Anki with FSRS]], written to the minimum-information principle. Card the facts; do not try to card the skill.
4. **Practise the capability and use feedback.** Choose guidance according to demonstrated prior knowledge; adjacent professional experience is not sufficient evidence of mastery. [[Project-based and just-in-time learning]] and [[Feedback, help, and learning with others]].
5. **For fuzzy judgment** (market selection, positioning, fundraising timing), inspect feedback quality rather than assuming experience will produce expertise. [[Deliberate practice in fuzzy domains]].
6. **Use AI with an independent check.** It can explain, provide examples, or quiz; assess what can subsequently be done without it. [[Learning with AI]].
7. **Arrange later attempts and protect sleep.** [[The spacing effect]] and [[Sleep and consolidation]].

## Strength of the evidence

- **Retention:** retrieval and spacing have substantial experimental and review evidence; specific schedules and software claims require separate support.
- **Instruction and correction:** worked examples, self-explanation, and informative feedback have evidence with task and expertise boundaries.
- **Execution:** environmental changes, if–then plans, monitoring, and breaks have different populations, measures, and levels of support; use the qualifications in the linked notes.
- **Professional judgment:** domain validity and feedback quality constrain what can be inferred from accumulated experience.

See [[What the science can and can't give you]] for why these findings do not identify a universal optimum or make knowledge, instruction, and learning conditions interchangeable.
