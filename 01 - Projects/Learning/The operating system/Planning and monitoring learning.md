# Planning and monitoring learning

The practical gap between choosing a topic and studying it is deciding what performance would count as learning, checking the current level, and changing the plan in response. This makes the planning–monitoring–reflection cycle in [[Metacognition and self-regulated learning]] concrete.

## Monitor the outcome the goal actually requires

Harkin et al. (2016) synthesised 138 randomised studies of interventions encouraging progress monitoring. They found improved goal attainment on average, *d* ≈ 0.40. Effects were larger when progress was recorded or reported, although such moderator comparisons do not by themselves establish why. The studies covered varied goals, not only learning. [Meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025).

**Application:** make the learning outcome observable and record attempts at it. For example, “write and explain a SQL join without copying an example” makes it possible to inspect performance. “Spend two hours on SQL” records participation but does not by itself test that capability. This choice of example and log format is an application of monitoring and [[The metacognitive trap|calibration]] research, not a validated curriculum.

| Intended capability | An illustrative check |
|---|---|
| Recall essential facts | Answer relevant questions without the source. |
| Explain a mechanism | Explain why it works and check the explanation against the source. |
| Execute a procedure | Perform a representative task without step-by-step guidance. |
| Apply knowledge in a new case | Attempt a changed problem, not only the practised example. |

These checks correspond to [[The testing effect]], [[Elaboration and self-explanation]], [[Learning to do, not just know]], and [[Transfer of learning]]. They are different outcomes; one cannot stand in for all the others.

## Use performance to choose guidance

Prior knowledge changes the value of instructional support (Kalyuga et al. 2003). **Application:** use an initial attempt to locate what is missing. If the task cannot be understood, begin with instruction or a worked example. If the method is understood but execution is unreliable, practise execution with correction. This is a practical use of the expertise-reversal finding, not a diagnostic score with a validated cutoff.

Being experienced in software or business does not establish expertise in every adjacent subject. The level of guidance should follow demonstrated knowledge of the task, not a general label such as “experienced founder.” See [[Project-based and just-in-time learning]].

## Schedule opportunities to retrieve again

Cepeda et al. (2006) synthesised distributed-practice experiments and found benefits for retaining verbal material when learning opportunities were separated in time. This supports allocating later encounters, not a universal calendar for every subject. [Meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354).

**Application:** after an initial session, arrange another attempt at the relevant questions or task on a later occasion. Choose intervals in relation to the retention goal and actual recall; [[The spacing effect]] covers the qualifications. A planner entry cannot compensate for repeating an ineffective activity.

## A minimal record

An illustrative implementation of the above findings:

> Intended capability → unaided attempt → error or missing knowledge → guidance or practice needed → next attempt.

The components have supporting research. This exact record, its frequency, and the combined workflow have not been validated as a package.
