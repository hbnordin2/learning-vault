# Start here

This vault is a set of notes that an AI assistant can read and add to. You keep reading and writing notes in Obsidian, as you would anyway. The assistant does the slower work around them: it rewrites papers into plain English, turns notes into podcasts for the phone, writes practice worksheets, and helps you remember what matters with flashcards.

There are three ways to use it. Most people use all three, for different moments.

## 1. In Obsidian, on the laptop or the phone

Obsidian is where the notes live. Everything here is an ordinary text file, so nothing is locked inside an app, and every change is saved to your private GitHub copy. That copy is how the phone and both laptops stay in step: edit a note on the phone, and it is on the laptop a few minutes later.

Worksheets work here too. Open one, switch to editing mode (the pencil icon), and click an answer. The worksheet marks it right or wrong and shows the explanation. Your answers are saved in the note, which is how the next worksheet knows what to ask again.

## 2. With an agent on the laptop

An agent is an assistant that can work with files and run programs, not just chat. Codex (from OpenAI), Claude Code (from Anthropic) and Cursor are all agents, and all of them can work in this vault. Open one in the vault folder and ask in plain words:

- "Write up this paper" (drag in a PDF, or paste a DOI or link)
- "Make me a 45-minute podcast on the spacing effect"
- "Make a podcast of this paper"
- "Give me a worksheet on the Foundations folder"
- "I finished the worksheet"
- "Help me really understand retrieval practice"
- "Review my Anki cards"
- "Is this draft clear?" (with a grant section, a handout, a paper draft)

The agent knows how to do each of these because of the skills in the vault: instruction files it reads when a request matches. You do not need to know their names. Only an agent on the laptop can make podcasts, write notes and save changes. The chat apps below can only read.

## 3. In ChatGPT or the Claude app, including by voice

The chat apps can read your notes and talk them through with you, out loud if you like, on the phone or the sofa. With the study modes, you can say "tutor me on interleaving" or "quiz me on the testing effect", and the app runs a proper tutoring session or a spoken worksheet instead of just chatting.

[[Talking to your notes in ChatGPT or Claude]] explains the one-time setup. [[Study modes for chat apps]] holds the instructions to paste in, and explains each mode.

## Podcasts

A podcast episode is two voices talking through one idea for 45 to 60 minutes, or through a whole paper in 15 to 20. They are written for listening while you do something else. The hosts keep coming back to the main idea, so drifting off for a few minutes loses nothing. New episodes appear in the podcast app on your phone, in a show called "Vault". The transcript of each one is saved in `01 - Projects/Learning/Podcasts/`.

## Remembering

Understanding something today does not mean it will still be there next month. [[Flashcards and Anki, explained]] covers how the vault helps with that, and why flashcards are only made for things you have already understood.

## Where the learning research is

`01 - Projects/Learning/` holds about seventy notes on what research says about learning and memory, with the evidence and its limits for each. The assistant uses them as its reference when it designs a worksheet or a podcast. They are also worth reading. [[How to learn]] is one continuous essay and the easiest way in. [[Learning]] is the map of the whole folder.

## Something not working?

Ask the agent on the laptop: "Something is wrong with the vault: <what you saw>". If a piece was skipped during setup, say "finish the setup in SETUP.md".
